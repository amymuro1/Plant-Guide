How to Tell When You Are Overwatering Your Houseplant

Have you noticed gnats flying around?

Has the soil become waterlogged?

Are the leaves turning yellow quickly?

These are common signs your plant is being overwatered.

What Is Considered Overwatering?

Overwatering occurs when you water your plant too frequently in a span of a few days. You can also have issues of stagnant water in the pot, if you water your plant and don't provide drainage holes.

Signs of Overwatering

  • Waterlogged soil: Soil that is constantly wet and does not dry out between waterings.
  • Fungus gnats: These small flying insects are attracted to damp soil and can be a sign of overwatering.
  • Quick yellowing of leaves: Leaves may turn yellow and drop if they are overwatered due to root rot.

Yellow leaves indicating overwatering, and presence of fungus gnats are common signs of overwatering.

What Happens If You Overwater Your Plant Too Frequently?

The main problem with overwatering is that it can lead to root rot, which is a fungal disease that affects the roots. The roots will become mushy and black, and the plant will die. The stem of the plant will also become mushy, and the leaves will turn yellow and drop.

This disease will kill your plant in a matter of weeks if not treated.

So, What Can You Do to Prevent Overwatering?

  1. When you receive or buy a new plant, check that the nursery pot it's in has drainage holes. If it does not, you can drill some holes in the bottom of the pot, or repot the plant into a pot that has drainage holes.
  2. Allow the top inch of soil to dry out between waterings. You can use a skewer or your finger to check the soil moisture.
  3. Every once in a while allow for aeration in your room by turning on a fan on a low setting. This will help the soil dry out faster, and is crucial for areas with numerous plants.
